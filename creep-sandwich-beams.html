B.Sc. Thesis Project

Time-Dependent Bending Response of a Composite Sandwich Beam with a Viscoelastic Core

Finite Element Study of Creep Behavior under Sustained Loading Using ANSYS

Reza Talaneh  |  B.Sc. Thesis, Summer 2013  |  Babol Noshirvani University of Technology
Supervisor: Prof. R. A. Alashti

Download Full Thesis Report (fa) Back to Home

Overview

Sandwich beams, consisting of thin, stiff skins bonded to a lightweight core, are widely used in aerospace, marine, and automotive structures. Because polymer-based cores can exhibit time-dependent (viscoelastic) behavior, this project studied the bending response of a sandwich beam with orthotropic graphite-epoxy skins and a viscoelastic polymer core under sustained loading, using the finite element software ANSYS.

Approach

  • Modeled the skins and core with 3D SOLID186 elements, assigning orthotropic elastic properties to the skins and an implicit Modified Time Hardening creep law to the core.
  • Solved the problem in two steps: an initial static response, followed by a long-duration creep analysis under sustained end loading (500 N and 1000 N), tracking displacement and strain over time.
  • Reached this formulation after testing and ruling out several earlier element combinations (SHELL, and combined SHELL/PLANE/SOLID models), which failed to correctly transfer strain between the skins and the core.

Result

Under sustained loading, both the tip displacement and the creep strain at the free end increased with time, consistent with the expected viscoelastic creep behavior of the polymer core. Results were recorded for load levels of 500 N and 1000 N over time scales spanning from near-instantaneous loading to long-term sustained loading.

Methods & Tools

ANSYS (SOLID186, implicit creep formulation) Orthotropic composite material modeling Viscoelastic (creep) analysis Finite Element Analysis
