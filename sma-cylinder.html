M.Sc. Thesis Project

Superelastic & Shape-Memory Behavior of Thick-Walled SMA Cylinders

Analytical & Finite Element Study of Ni-Ti Tubes under Internal Pressure and Combined Pressure-Torsion Loading

Reza Talaneh  |  M.Sc. Thesis, 2016  |  Babol Noshirvani University of Technology
Supervisor: Prof. R. A. Alashti  •  Advisor: Prof. A. Fathi

Download Full Thesis Report Back to Home

Overview

Shape memory alloys recover large strains through a reversible solid-state phase transformation and are widely used as actuators, including in cylindrical torque-tube and pressure-vessel geometries. This thesis studied the superelastic and shape-memory behavior of a thick-walled Ni-Ti cylinder, first under internal pressure alone, then under combined internal pressure and torsion.

Approach

  • Reduced the 3D Boyd-Lagoudas thermomechanical constitutive model to the plane-stress state governing the cylinder wall for each loading case.
  • Derived the stress-based compatibility equation and discretized it with a finite-difference scheme, solving the resulting nonlinear algebraic system in MATLAB.
  • Modeled both loading cases in Abaqus using 3D solid elements and an existing Fortran user-material subroutine for the SMA constitutive model as an independent check.

Result

For internal pressure alone, the finite-difference solution closely matched published solutions. For combined pressure-torsion loading, for which no independent published solution was available, the finite-difference results closely matched the three-dimensional Abaqus simulations. In both cases, the martensitic phase transformation was found to initiate at the inner radius and propagate outward with increasing load, producing a corresponding redistribution of the radial, circumferential, and shear stresses across the wall. Superposed torsion was shown to alter this stress and transformation pattern relative to the pure-pressure case.

Outlook

As one application, the thick-walled model was used to examine a self-locking SMA pipe coupling: an oversized SMA sleeve fitted over two steel pipe ends recovers its residual strain on heating, generating contact pressure between the sleeve and the pipes that persists on subsequent cooling. Building on this and the combined pressure-torsion results, the thesis outlines several directions for future work: extending the analysis to plane-strain thick-walled cylinders; using the combined-loading results to study angular-misalignment effects and the design of SMA power-transmission shaft couplings; applying the present method to other two-dimensional SMA problems such as beam bending and rotating disks; and examining residual stresses under differing inner/outer radial displacement, as well as the dynamic response of the cylinder.
